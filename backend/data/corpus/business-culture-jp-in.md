# Business culture: Japanese and Indian counterparts

_Starter summary written from general knowledge, not quoted from the source. Verify against the official source before relying on it._

Japanese firms tend to decide by consensus (nemawashi and ringi), which is slow to start and fast to execute, and communicate indirectly, so 'we will consider it' often means no. Indian firms are often led by a founder or family head who can decide quickly, communicate more directly, and treat relationships and flexibility as part of the deal; 'no problem' can express willingness rather than a checked commitment. Meetings in India may start late or run long. Practical habits that help both sides: written minutes after every meeting, explicit dates and owners, regular reporting, and early agreement on how escalation works. These are tendencies, not rules about individuals.
